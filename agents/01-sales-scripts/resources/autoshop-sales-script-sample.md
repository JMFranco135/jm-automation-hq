🔹 Likely Objections & Handles

To see if distance is actually the problem:

Just to understand where you're at and make sure I understand you. If we truly are the best mechanic for the job you're looking for, would it make sense for you to make that drive to (painpoint) or it still wouldn't.


Handle:
 “Totally fair — a lot of people who reach out aren’t in ‘buy now’ mode, they’re just exploring. Out of curiosity, what were you hoping to find out when you responded? Was it more about preventing problems down the road, or solving something specific right now?”
(Miner-style question — shifts them from “no interest” → articulating a reason themselves. Then tie back to the free inspection as a safe first step.)
Handle:
 “I get it — most people are slammed. That is why we keep the inspection real quick, so we can prevent hassle or unexpected bills later. Whats a morning or afternoon that would be be easier for you?”

Handle:
 “Great question — and I’d be wondering the same thing if I were in your shoes! The inspection is 100% free, no strings. The reason we do it is simple: we win when people experience our service, because 9 times out of 10 they come back.
So it’s more of an investment on our side — a way to earn your trust before you spend a dime.”
(Hormozi frame → transparency + math of why it makes sense. Builds trust instead of hype.)
Handle:
 “Totally makes sense — most of our clients had a shop they went to before finding us. Out of curiosity, what do you like most about them? And if there was one thing you wish they did differently, what would that be?”
(Cole-style: acknowledges loyalty, then probes for gaps. If gap appears, position free inspection as a no-risk way to compare.)
Handle:
 “Of course, makes sense to be on the same page. Let me ask — if they were here right now and knew that the inspection was free and could potentially prevent a costly repair down the road, do you think they’d be on board with at least checking it out?
We can even schedule a time that works for both of you.”
(Respectful, non-pushy, keeps the momentum alive.)
Handle:
 “Yeah, I get that — and honestly, that’s why we flipped the model. The inspection is free, and the goal is just to show you if there’s anything urgent you should know about. If everything looks good, great — you’ll have peace of mind. If something does need attention, you’ll have the info and you can choose what to do, when to do it, and with who.
No push, just clarity.”
(Miner + Hormozi style → defuses fear with transparency, positions you as trusted advisor.)
Handle:
 “Great question. Honestly, you shouldn’t switch unless it actually makes sense. That’s why we do the inspection first — so you can see how we treat people, how thorough we are, and then you decide if it’s a better fit.
It’s kind of like test driving a car — you don’t commit until you feel it for yourself.”
(Future pacing + “you decide” lowers resistance, positions you as confident in the product.)


“I understand wanting to wait. The only thing I’d mention is that with performance vehicles especially, problems don’t always show up when the mileage says they should. That’s why we offer the free inspection — it costs nothing now, but it could save you thousands later.”

For people who haven't hit service inspection yet:
“I get that — the mileage interval is just a guideline, but with German vehicles especially, we see things like small leaks, worn bushings, or cooling issues start before that mark. That’s exactly why we offer the free inspection — it’s not about selling you something early, it’s about preventing a $2,000–$5,000 repair down the road.”
